# Language information and discovery

Every language in the catalogue has a short introduction, example use areas and links to primary documentation. These descriptions are separate from suitability, performance and complexity assessments. The 159 unassessed identities retain their pinned IDs, UNKNOWN assessments and NOT_REVIEWED status.

## Finding languages

Search matches names, introductions, use areas and scoped lifecycle notes. Three independent filters and six sorts help explore the catalogue:

- **Usage:** in or outside the cited GitHub top 10.
- **Documented origins:** before 1990, 1990 onward, or year unverified.
- **Implementation lifecycle:** maintained, experimental, retired/deprecated, or unverified.
- **Sort:** A–Z, Z–A, common first, oldest/newest documented origins, or implementation status.

Combine filters to rediscover classics or find older languages with maintained implementations. Direct profile links clear conflicting discovery filters. View links preserve search, filters and sort; browser Back/Forward restores them. Discovery controls apply only to languages.

## What the labels mean

Usage comes from [GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/), measured by monthly contributors in August 2025. It is a dated GitHub observation, not a global popularity or quality score. The report's Shell aggregate is not assigned to Bash, so nine exact catalogue identities match its top 10. Being outside this list does not imply a niche, forgotten or inactive language.

Origin years describe documented milestones: design, implementation, standardization or publication. They are not uniform first-release dates. The 1990 boundary is a navigation convention. Unknown years sort last; open the linked historical source for context.

Lifecycle describes the named implementation or project in its evidence note. Maintained means explicit ongoing support or recent release evidence; experimental means the source explicitly says so. Retired/deprecated identifies a scoped archive or deprecation notice, not extinction of a language family. Missing evidence remains unverified. Historical age and maintenance are independent.

## Data and sources

The checked date and primary links appear in each card's **Sources and context** disclosure. The static dataset combines [shared metadata and A–F](../catalog/language-information.v1.json) with alphabetic records for [G–P](../catalog/language-information-g-p.v1.json) and [Q–Z](../catalog/language-information-q-z.v1.json). All parts are validated together; no live API, account or background fetching is required.

Validation requires complete catalogue coverage, unique identities, HTTPS references, and linked evidence for concrete years and lifecycle labels. Descriptive information never changes Reviewed decision eligibility or creates ratings.
